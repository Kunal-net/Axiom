5. Termination and Notice

5.1 This Agreement will commence on the Effective Date and will continue until the Services are completed in accordance with the Statement of Work, unless earlier terminated as permitted under this Section 5.

CLAUSE 5.2 • HIGH RISK priority_high
“The Client may terminate this Agreement at any time, for any reason, without prior notice and without liability.”

5.3 Upon any early cessation under Section 5.1, Contractor shall promptly cease all performance and deliver to Client all partially completed Work Product and documentation up to the effective notice timestamp.

6. Intellectual Property & Deliverables

6.1 Work for Hire. Freelancer agrees that all deliverables, inventions, discoveries, and copyrightable works created solely or jointly during performance shall be considered a "work made for hire" for the sole benefit of Client.

6.2 Assignment of Rights. In the event any materials do not qualify as work made for hire, Freelancer irrevocably transfers, assigns, and sets over to Client all right, title, and interest throughout the universe.

High Risk Clause

Unilateral termination

Found in Section 5.2 on Page 4
Original clause

“The Client may terminate this Agreement at any time, for any reason, without prior notice and without liability.”

Plain English explanation

This clause allows the client to end the contract at any time without notice or compensation, which may be unfair and leaves you financially vulnerable after investing time.

Why this is a risk
  • cancel No prior notice period: Work can be halted mid-sprint without advance warning or transition plan.
  • cancel No compensation for completed work: Unbilled or in-progress labor may go entirely unpaid.
  • cancel Gives full control to the client: Creates an asymmetric legal balance between parties with zero freelance recourse.
Suggested revision Fair Standard

“Either party may terminate this Agreement with 30 days’ written notice. The Client shall compensate the Freelancer for all work completed up to the termination date.”
